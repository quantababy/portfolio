## Overview

VolatilityForge is a quantitative finance research project focused on forecasting market volatility and evaluating how those forecasts behave in systematic volatility-targeting strategies.

The project combines historical market-data processing, realized-variance forecasting, econometric baselines, machine-learning models, statistical forecast comparisons, and transaction-cost-aware backtesting.

The core asset is SPY, with VIX used as an implied-volatility reference. The forecasting system evaluates two horizons: next-day realized variance and cumulative realized variance over the next five trading days.

Rather than evaluating models solely on predictive error, VolatilityForge also investigates volatility regimes, strategy exposure, turnover, and net performance after transaction costs.

The project is designed around reproducible research and chronological evaluation. Model training and forecast evaluation use expanding-window walk-forward procedures, with safeguards intended to prevent future information from entering training or residual estimation.

## Architecture

The high-level workflow is:

1. Download and validate SPY and VIX market data.
2. Align both assets on shared trading dates.
3. Construct volatility features and forward realized-variance targets.
4. Generate forecasts using statistical and machine-learning models.
5. Evaluate forecasts using QLIKE and MSE.
6. Compare forecasts statistically and analyze volatility regimes.
7. Evaluate volatility-targeting strategies with transaction costs.
8. Produce research reports and interactive dashboard views.

### Market-data pipeline

The data pipeline downloads daily SPY and VIX data using `yfinance`.

Raw observations are validated and saved as Parquet files. The alignment stage joins both assets on normalized dates, while subsequent processing constructs volatility features and forward targets.

The pipeline is configured through `configs/config.yaml`.

Run the pipeline from the repository root with the project environment activated:

```bash
python -m src.run_data_pipeline
```

The pipeline produces:

- `data/raw/spy.parquet`
- `data/raw/vix.parquet`
- `data/processed/spy_vix.parquet`
- `data/processed/volatility_features.parquet`

The pipeline requires network access for market-data downloads. Its integration test mocks the download operation so that the test can run without network access.

Historical data can be revised by the provider. Rebuilding the pipeline therefore does not guarantee bit-for-bit reproducibility of previously downloaded observations.

### Volatility forecasting

VolatilityForge establishes statistical forecasting baselines before relying on more complex machine-learning models.

The implemented forecasting approaches include:

- **EWMA:** an exponentially weighted volatility estimate.
- **GARCH:** a conditional-volatility model.
- **GJR-GARCH:** a conditional-volatility model that accommodates asymmetric responses to shocks.
- **HAR-RV:** a heterogeneous autoregressive model for realized volatility.
- **LightGBM:** a gradient-boosting model for nonlinear forecasting relationships.

The forecasting workflow evaluates one-day and five-day horizons.

Ridge, Lasso, XGBoost, and a HAR-RV residual hybrid remain unverified as implemented in the current project documentation. They are not presented here as completed forecasting components.

HAR-RV is the primary benchmark for the forecasting research.

### Leakage-aware evaluation

The project uses chronological expanding-window walk-forward evaluation rather than randomly splitting time-series observations.

At each forecast origin, training data must satisfy the applicable information-availability constraints. Multi-day targets must have fully matured before they are eligible for training or residual estimation.

This is particularly important for five-day forecasts, where the outcome associated with a recent observation may not yet be fully observed.

The primary evaluation metric is QLIKE, with mean squared error (MSE) as a secondary metric.

Diebold–Mariano forecast comparisons and HAC-lag sensitivity analysis are also implemented to investigate differences between competing forecasts.

### Experimental HAR-RV smearing correction

A separate experiment investigates Duan's smearing correction for five-day HAR-RV forecasts.

The experiment estimates a multiplicative correction factor from historical out-of-sample log residuals. Only residuals whose five-day targets had fully matured before the forecast origin are eligible for estimation.

The correction is implemented separately from the production HAR-RV forecasting code and has not been adopted as the production model.

Keeping the experiment separate allows the research result to be evaluated without silently changing the established benchmark.

### Volatility-regime analysis

VolatilityForge includes volatility-regime analysis to investigate how forecasting behavior varies across realized-volatility conditions.

This analysis matters because an aggregate improvement can conceal deterioration in particular market regimes.

The smearing experiment, for example, improved aggregate metrics but showed weaker performance in the low-realized-variance regime. The project therefore treats aggregate scores as one part of model evaluation rather than sufficient evidence of consistent improvement.

### Strategy backtesting

The strategy layer evaluates volatility-targeting rules using the saved forecasts and processed feature data.

Each forecast made after the close on date *t* is applied to the next session's return. Exposure is long-only and capped by the configured maximum leverage. Transaction costs are charged on absolute changes in exposure.

The configured baseline strategy uses:

- Annualized volatility target: 10%
- Maximum leverage: 1.0
- Transaction costs: 5 basis points per unit of turnover

The strategy evaluation reports gross and net returns, annualized return and volatility, zero-risk-free-rate Sharpe ratio, maximum drawdown, exposure, and turnover.

The comparison includes a same-period gross buy-and-hold benchmark. Since this benchmark is gross while the strategy's net returns deduct transaction costs, the comparison is not cost-matched.

### Strategy sensitivity analysis

The sensitivity analysis evaluates five forecasting models across:

- Annualized volatility targets of 8%, 10%, and 12%.
- Transaction costs of 0, 5, 10, and 20 basis points.

The analysis reports net performance and turnover-related costs. It helps examine how results change under alternative strategy assumptions rather than relying on a single configuration.

The strategy reports can be reproduced from the repository root after the required processed features and forecast files have been generated:

```bash
python -m src.run_strategy_backtest
python -m src.run_strategy_report
python -m src.run_strategy_sensitivity
```

These commands run the configured backtests and generate:

- `reports/strategy_performance.csv`
- `reports/strategy_sensitivity.csv`

The report-generation commands reuse saved forecasts and processed data; they do not independently rebuild the data pipeline or retrain the forecasting models.

### Interactive research dashboard

The project includes a Streamlit dashboard for exploring research outputs.

Run it from the repository root with the virtual environment activated:

```bash
python -m streamlit run dashboard/app.py
```

The documented dashboard views cover the project overview, model comparisons, volatility regimes, and strategy backtests.

The dashboard presents research outputs for inspection; it does not establish that the models or strategies are suitable for live trading.

## Engineering decisions

### Statistical baselines before machine learning

EWMA, GARCH-family models, and HAR-RV establish reference points before more complex machine-learning approaches are considered.

This makes it possible to assess whether additional model complexity improves forecasting performance rather than assuming that it does.

### Walk-forward evaluation instead of random splitting

Financial time series have temporal dependencies, and forward targets can overlap with later observations.

Expanding-window walk-forward evaluation preserves chronological ordering and makes information availability at each forecast origin explicit.

### QLIKE as the primary metric

QLIKE is the primary forecast-comparison metric, while MSE provides a secondary view of forecast error.

Using both metrics helps avoid basing model selection on one measure alone. Statistical forecast comparisons provide additional context, although they do not guarantee economic usefulness.

### Separate forecast evaluation from strategy evaluation

A forecast with better statistical metrics does not necessarily produce a better net trading strategy.

VolatilityForge therefore evaluates predictive performance separately from exposure, turnover, transaction costs, drawdown, and net returns.

### Explicit development and prospective evaluation periods

The period beginning in 2022 has already been used for model comparisons, experimental corrections, regime analysis, and strategy backtests.

It is classified as development data, not as an untouched final holdout. A prospective evaluation period must be defined after the model specifications, features, hyperparameters, and evaluation protocol are frozen.

### Reproducibility with documented limitations

Configuration files, automated tests, saved research reports, and documented commands support repeatable experiments.

Reproducibility is not absolute: market-data providers may revise historical observations, and prospective performance remains unknown until an appropriate future evaluation is conducted.

## Results

### Experimental five-day HAR-RV comparison

The development-period smearing experiment produced the following results:

| Metric | Baseline HAR-RV | OOS-smearing HAR-RV |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean QLIKE | 0.457635 | 0.372767 |
| MSE | 1.017897 × 10⁻⁶ | 9.917346 × 10⁻⁷ |
| Observations | 1,196 | 1,196 |

Evaluation period: January 3, 2022, through October 8, 2026.

In this development sample, the correction improved aggregate QLIKE by approximately 18.54% and MSE by approximately 2.57%.

These results require important qualifications:

- The improvement was uneven across years and realized-variance regimes.
- Low-realized-variance regime performance deteriorated.
- QLIKE improved on 496 of the 1,196 individual dates.
- The experiment does not establish statistical significance or future performance.
- The correction remains experimental and has not been adopted in the production HAR-RV model.

The latest processed market-data date documented for this experiment is October 15, 2026. The latest valid five-day target is October 8, 2026, because the final five forecast origins do not yet have fully matured five-day outcomes.

These figures describe the supplied development-period experiment, not an independent prospective evaluation.

### Strategy research

The strategy implementation evaluates five forecasting models across the configured volatility-target and transaction-cost assumptions.

The resulting reports include net strategy performance, turnover-related costs, and a same-period gross buy-and-hold benchmark.

No specific strategy return, Sharpe ratio, drawdown, or outperformance claim is made in this case study because numerical strategy results have not been provided here for verification.

The backtests are historical research, not evidence of live executability or future profitability.

## What broke / constraints

### Market-data reproducibility

Historical market data can be revised by the provider. Re-running the data pipeline may therefore produce different files even with the same configuration.

### Forecast-target maturity

Multi-day realized-variance targets require future observations to mature. Training or estimating corrections from targets that are not yet fully observed would introduce information-availability problems.

### Aggregate metrics can hide regime deterioration

The experimental smearing correction improved aggregate QLIKE and MSE, but its improvement was not uniform. This is why regime analysis and date-level comparisons remain important.

### Statistical improvements do not guarantee economic improvements

Forecast accuracy and trading performance are different objectives. Transaction costs, turnover, leverage constraints, and exposure timing affect the strategy results.

### Development-period reuse

The evaluation period beginning in 2022 has already influenced research decisions and strategy analysis. It cannot be treated as an untouched final holdout.

### Prospective performance remains unknown

No prospective final evaluation has been established in the supplied project status. Historical findings should not be interpreted as evidence that the same performance will persist.
